# UK compliance basics for social video ads

A working checklist, not legal advice. When something is new or borderline, flag it to the director rather than guess; the ASA's CAP Copy Advice service gives free pre-publication guidance.

## Prices
- Consumer prices include VAT and any fees everyone has to pay (CAP Code rule 3.18).
- Price maths on screen ("3 for £36") must be correct at the advertised price, and any "was" price must be a genuine previous price.
- The Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (Part 4, Chapter 1) replaced the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 from 6 April 2025. Misleading actions and omissions are still banned, and the CMA can now fine businesses directly.

## "Free" claims (CAP Code rules 3.23 to 3.26)
- "Buy 2, get the 3rd free" works when the two paid tickets are at their usual price and the third is genuinely free. Never raise the paid price to cover it.
- Make the commitment clear: new customers only, sign-up needed, maximum group size.

## Promotions (CAP Code section 8)
- Show the significant conditions in the ad or signpost them clearly: how to claim, start and closing date, who qualifies, limits (rule 8.17). The end card's terms line exists for this.
- Promoters must avoid causing unnecessary disappointment (rule 8.2). An early-end clause such as "We reserve the right to end this offer sooner" can be included, but use it rarely and fairly: honour bookings and claims already made, announce any new end date on the website and social channels in advance, and never use it to create false urgency.
- Urgency must be true: "ends Sunday" only if it really ends Sunday.

## Children (CAP Code section 5)
- Children's voices and handwriting (the notebook series) are fine in ads aimed at adults. Target 18+ on Meta, never urge children to buy or to pester their parents, and never show unsafe behaviour.
- Photos of children need documented consent for marketing use (UK GDPR). STEMEX works with children and vulnerable young people: follow its safeguarding policy, and when unsure use hands, backs of heads or no children at all.

## Food brands (Muffin Break, Cafe UFO)
- The UK restrictions on advertising less healthy food and drink (a ban on paid online ads and a 9pm TV watershed, in force from 5 January 2026) can apply to ads showing products such as cakes, muffins, pastries, fried food and sugary drinks. Businesses with fewer than 250 employees are generally exempt, franchise arrangements can change how that is counted, and brand-only ads with no identifiable product are treated differently. Check with the franchisor (Foodco for Muffin Break) or get legal advice before running product-led paid ads.
- No health or nutrition claims ("healthy", "guilt-free") unless they are authorised, and no allergen claims without evidence.
- Muffin Break is a franchise: the franchisor's rules on logos, offers, pricing and approvals come before anything we design.

## Music, fonts and images
- Music: licensed tracks only (`music.md`). Fonts: Google Fonts or fonts the brand has licensed. Photos: the brand's own, with consent for any people shown.
- No other brands' logos or licensed characters. Generic seasonal figures are fine.

## Meta advertising standards
- Do not assert or imply the viewer's personal attributes, such as age, health or finances: "A day out for the whole family" is fine, "You're over 65, so..." is not.
- No sensational claims, no misleading buttons, and the landing page must match the offer.
- The ad copy, the video and the landing page must all show the same offer and terms.
